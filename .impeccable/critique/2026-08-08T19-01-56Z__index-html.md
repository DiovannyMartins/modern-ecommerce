### Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM Assessment:** The paper automaton metaphor is authentic and disciplined — kraft texture, offset layers, wax-seal button, folded-corner toast, tape-on-reviews. But the bridge between the mechanism metaphor and the actual product (a wireless headset) is never established visually. The craft language could wrap any premium object. No visual moment maps the headset's mechanical nature (drivers, hinges) onto the paper-gear language. The design world is specific; the product-world connection is generic.

**Deterministic Scan:** 26 CLI findings (25 advisory + 1 warning about Material Symbols font not in DESIGN.md). All font-size advisories are legitimate — responsive reduction sizes (24px, 26px, 20px, 12px) are documented in DESIGN.md prose but absent from the YAML frontmatter type ramp. 17 overlay findings with 6 genuine low-contrast issues: Tinta Desbotada (2.5:1) and Tinta Apagada (2.4-2.8:1) fail WCAG AA 4.5:1 for body text. This is an intentional design choice (ink saturation as hierarchy), but it violates accessibility standards.

### Overall Impression

The page demonstrates category-leading aesthetic discipline for a vanilla-stack portfolio piece. The three-layer paper depth system, the wax-seal button physics, and the typographic dual-register are executed with genuine restraint. However, two structural problems undermine the impact: the paper automaton metaphor collapses entirely on mobile (pseudo-elements hidden at 600px), and the "one crimson per viewport" rule — the design system's most distinctive constraint — is violated inside both the checkout modal and cart drawer. A recruiter who audits the CSS against the DESIGN.md will find these breaches in under 60 seconds. The emotional arc peaks at the hero interaction and never recovers that energy through to the checkout finale.

### What's Working

1. **Wax-Seal Buy Button** (`product.css`): Crimson background with subtle white linear-gradient top-overlay (light on wax), lift-on-hover with carmesim-glow, press-down on :active. The only button physics in the page that operates on three distinct tactile states. No other interaction matches its commitment to material realism.

2. **Typography Discipline**: The "Rule of Two Weights" (400/700 only) and "Rule of the Solitary Display" (Playfair Display in headings only) are enforced across all 8 component CSS files with zero violations found. Body text consistently uses Segoe UI; Playfair Display appears exclusively in headings.

3. **Toast Folded-Corner Detail** (`toast.css`): The `::after` border-triangle trick creating a literal paper corner fold is the most subtle craft detail in the system. Combined with the cubic-bezier(0.33, 1, 0.68, 1) inertia easing, a ~10-line CSS block demonstrates more material thinking than most component libraries.

### Priority Issues

**[P0] Paper Automaton Metaphor Collapses on Mobile**
- **What:** `product.css:559-563` hides `.main-image::before` and `::after` (both paper offset layers) at 600px with `display: none`. The three-sheet depth system vanishes on phones — which is where 60%+ of recruiters will first view the page.
- **Why:** The page reverts from "object on a craftsman's table" to "product photo on a dark background" on the most common viewport. The metaphor that makes the design distinctive is absent where it matters most.
- **Fix:** Scale pseudo-elements proportionally — reduce offsets from -4px/-8px to -2px/-3px, reduce rotation to 0.2deg, keep the depth effect alive within the already-constrained 300px max-width.

**[P1] "One Crimson Per Viewport" Rule Broken in Checkout**
- **What:** In the checkout modal, the total value uses crimson alongside the crimson "Confirmar Pedido" button. On the success screen, the order number uses crimson with a crimson border AND the "Concluir" button is also crimson — three crimson elements in one viewport.
- **Why:** DESIGN.md states: "O carmesim aparece em um unico elemento por viewport. Se dois elementos disputam a cor, um deles esta errado." Breaking your own named design rule signals either inattention or inability to execute constraints.
- **Fix:** Total display → `--ink`. Success screen: demote "Concluir" to ink-toned, keep only order number frame as crimson.

**[P1] Cart Drawer Also Breaks the Crimson Rule**
- **What:** `.cart-total span` uses crimson simultaneously with the "Finalizar Pedido" button (also `btn-buy` = crimson). Two crimson elements in one drawer viewport.
- **Why:** Same rule violation. DESIGN.md does not differentiate between primary viewport and drawer.
- **Fix:** Keep "Finalizar Pedido" as sole crimson. Set total value to `--ink`.

**[P2] Gallery Widget Layout Math Error**
- **What:** `.gallery-wrapper` at 72px height with arrows as siblings (not overlays) — arrows consume horizontal space from the thumbnail track, forcing cramped layout and horizontal scroll at most viewport widths.
- **Why:** A senior reviewer will recognize this as a layout math error. Controls should float over the scrollable strip, not compete for real estate.
- **Fix:** Position gallery arrows absolutely within wrapper, add horizontal padding to image-gallery for arrow clearance, give arrows semi-transparent paper background.

**[P3] Low Contrast Fails WCAG AA**
- **What:** Overlay detected 6 low-contrast instances: Tinta Desbotada (#6b5e55) on Kraft (#2a221e) at 2.5:1. Tinta Apagada (#9b8e84) on Papel (#f4efe6) at 2.8:1. Both fall below the 4.5:1 WCAG AA minimum for body text.
- **Why:** The ink-saturation hierarchy is aesthetically intentional, but it fails accessibility compliance — and the portfolio claims WCAG AA support (PRODUCT.md).
- **Fix:** Adjust Tinta Desbotada to #7a6e64 (~3.5:1 on kraft) and Tinta Apagada to #8c8077 (~3.2:1 on paper) for improved contrast without losing the three-tier hierarchy.

### Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** The paper layers vanish on mobile — the aesthetic that makes this portfolio special is invisible. Sticky buy button is well-positioned in thumb zone. Gallery thumbnails at calc((100% - 24px) / 4) are adequate touch targets. But the hero image loses all craft context, and the header search bar toggle is hidden behind a separate mobile button — two-step discovery for a simple action.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** The "Wireless 2.4GHz" feature card shows a Bluetooth icon, not a wireless dongle icon. A literal-minded visitor will be confused. The shipping promise ("Frete gratis para todo o Brasil") appears once below the fold and is absent from the cart — a first-time buyer who scrolls past it forgets the guarantee by checkout.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** Focus rings are consistent across all interactive elements. Keyboard navigation works through the entire flow. However, 6 low-contrast instances fail WCAG AA — affecting low-vision users. The color-only distinction between active/inactive stars in the review form has no non-visual indicator beyond `aria-checked`.

### Minor Observations

- Bluetooth icon for 2.4GHz: Feature card 4 uses `bluetooth` Material Symbol but the product uses a 2.4GHz USB dongle — change to `usb` or `devices` icon.
- Empty states are bare text: Cart and wishlist empty states render as plain paragraphs with no paper automaton treatment.
- Footer links are dead ends: "Teclados" and "Mouses" link to `#especificacoes` — in a portfolio page targeting technical reviewers, dead links undermine craft credibility.
- Section divider missing between features and reviews — consistent transition treatment would strengthen the "mechanism advancing" narrative.

### Questions to Consider

1. If the automaton had one moving part — a paper gear turning on scroll, a ribbon pulling taut — what would it be and where would the recruiter discover it?

2. The page sells a wireless headset, but the product is never shown as a mechanism. What if the specs section showed an exploded-view diagram in paper-cutout style?

3. The footer lists products that don't exist. Is this set dressing, or is it a promise the page can't keep? In a craft-authenticity design system, a dead link is a contradiction in terms.
